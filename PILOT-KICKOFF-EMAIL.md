I hope Monday went smoothly, and that you and Brenda are settling into 1230.

Watching this week's office shuffle, with all the "Nathan has to move out before Aimee can move in," is what pushed me to finish something I've been building. Sorting a fair, humane set of assignments out of those dependencies is the kind of problem I've spent my career on, and you did it by hand, over weeks. I wanted software that could do the same arithmetic in seconds, every semester, and show each person why they got the office they got.

An office is earned by your teaching schedule, and the same rule applies to everyone. You say which days you're on campus. It pairs people whose days never overlap, so two colleagues can share one office and each still has it to themselves on the days they're in. If two people want to trade, they can, but only when everyone affected agrees. And nothing is ever official until the faculty vote to adopt the rule; until then, every run is just a simulation.

I didn't fully trust our own tool at first, so we fed it the real teaching schedule, all 120 who taught in person last spring, under invented names, and it matched our hand count to the digit: 61 offices needed, 60 available, short by one, and it named who.

I also want to go first, and now I can do it for real. As I move over to Sargent, I'd like to put my office into the system as a shared room anyone can book, and book it like everyone else when I need it. If we're going to ask colleagues to share, the first office in the pool should be mine.
