# SBS Faculty Space — Pilot Brief

*For Dean Riley. Five minutes to read; fifteen to see working.*

## Where we are

Since July, the office-sharing initiative has produced three things, in order:

1. **The numbers** (you have these): 120 SBS faculty taught in person last
   spring; at the busiest moment ~35 needed a seat outside a classroom; our
   Mon/Wed and Tue/Thu schedules naturally interleave. The building roughly
   fits — the hard part was never capacity, it's fairness and buy-in.
2. **The rule**: a plain-language charter faculty can ratify *before anyone
   knows their own room* — with hard guarantees (a real office standard,
   protected private rooms for confidential conversations, storage, open
   Fridays, human appeal) and honest limits (what the schedule can't measure,
   flagged, never hidden).
3. **The working system** — this is new. Not a mockup: real software, running
   the full loop your email described.

## What the system does today

- A faculty member **declares their semester** — days on campus, an optional
  preferred officemate — with the promise built into the screen: *the form
  shapes scheduling, never entitlement*. Offices are earned by the measured
  teaching schedule, so honesty is always the right answer.
- **The allocation runs**: colleagues who chose each other are paired first;
  everyone else is matched so paired schedules never collide (and the match is
  mathematically checked — no two people would both rather have swapped);
  everyone else gets a guaranteed seat, same room, all semester. Assignments
  appear on a shared calendar. If the rule can't place everyone, it says so —
  **with names** — instead of quietly squeezing anyone. That alarm goes to you,
  never to a faculty member.
- Every person sees **their office and the reasons for it**, clause by clause.
- **Swaps by consent**: any two colleagues can trade — and it only happens when
  everyone sharing the affected offices agrees. No one's officemate changes
  without their yes.
- **Your three decisions are dials in the product**, marked UNDECIDED until you
  set them — and every change requires a written reason that lands in a
  permanent log. The system refuses quiet edits, and it refuses to make any
  assignment "official" until the rule has actually been ratified by faculty.
- **The utilization picture you asked for**: faculty on campus by day, why
  sharing works, and how many offices sharing frees — space that can become
  student space.

**Proof it's real:** we loaded the actual Spring 2026 registrar schedule —
all 120 faculty, under invented names — and the system produced exactly the
numbers from the analysis you have: 61 offices needed against 60, short by
one, with the shortfall named. The analysis and the product agree to the digit.

## The fifteen-minute walkthrough

1. Sign in as a faculty member → declare a week → see the office reveal and
   its reasons. *(2 min)*
2. Propose a swap → watch it wait for every affected colleague's consent.
   *(2 min)*
3. Sign in as the Dean → your three dials → run the allocation on the real
   Spring schedules → watch it come up short by one, with names. *(5 min)*
4. Change a dial without a reason → watch the system refuse. Change it with
   one → watch it land in the log. *(3 min)*
5. Insights: the by-day picture, and the offices-freed number. *(3 min)*

Everyone in the demo is fictitious; the schedules are real; nothing in it
assigns anyone anything.

## What a fall pilot looks like

**A shadow pilot: we pilot the system, not the move.** Nobody changes offices.

- **Now–mid-Aug** — you set (or explicitly defer) the three dials; charter
  comment window opens with the plain-language packet and the spring
  simulation attached.
- **Late Aug** — charter ratified (or amended by the comments — both outcomes
  are the process working).
- **September** — one or two departments declare their real semesters in the
  system; the allocation runs in simulation alongside reality; faculty see
  what they *would* have, and tell us what's wrong.
- **End of term** — feedback becomes charter amendments (logged, reasoned),
  and we decide together whether spring goes live.
